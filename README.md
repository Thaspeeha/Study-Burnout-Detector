# Study-Burnout-Detector

input - I feel exhausted and unmotivated
input - pressured to do a research paper in 10 days

burnout warning sgins - Here's What I'm Noticing 💙
First, I just want to say — what you're feeling makes complete sense. Carrying a big deadline while already running on empty is genuinely hard, and it takes self-awareness to even pause and check in with yourself like this. That matters.

Here are a few things I gently noticed from what you shared:

Your energy feels depleted before the work even begins. Feeling exhausted going into a demanding task — rather than after it — can sometimes be a sign that your mind and body are already running low on reserves.

Motivation seems to have quietly slipped away. When the drive to do things you normally would do starts to fade, that's often your inner self waving a little flag, asking for rest and care.

The pressure feels time-compressed and intense. A 10-day deadline for a research paper is genuinely a lot. When big demands stack on top of an already tired mind, it can make everything feel heavier than it normally would.

The combination of exhaustion + low motivation + high pressure is a pattern worth paying attention to — these three together are some of the earliest whispers of burnout.

💛 A gentle note, just from me to you:

You don't have to push through this completely alone. Please consider reaching out to a counselor, a trusted friend, a family member, or your school's student support services. You deserve a soft place to land right now — not just a finish line to cross. You are so much more than your productivity. 🌿

recovery plan - Your Recovery Plan 💙
First, I want you to know — feeling exhausted and unmotivated doesn't mean you're failing. It means you're human, and you've been pushing hard. Here's a gentle plan to help you move forward without burning out completely:

1. Break the paper into tiny, non-scary pieces
Don't think "10-day research paper." Think "today I will write one paragraph" or "today I find 3 sources." Small daily targets feel manageable and actually build momentum faster than you'd expect.

2. Protect at least one hour of real rest daily
Not scrolling — actual rest. A walk, a nap, music, whatever genuinely recharges you. This isn't wasted time; it's what makes the work possible.

3. Start with just 25 minutes of focused work
Try the Pomodoro method — 25 minutes on, 5 minutes off. When you're depleted, starting is the hardest part. Give yourself permission to stop after one round if needed. You'll often keep going.

4. Say no to one unnecessary obligation this week
One thing. Guard your limited energy like it's precious — because right now, it genuinely is.

5. Talk to someone you trust
Even a five-minute conversation with a friend can lift the weight of isolation that exhaustion creates.

6. Celebrate small wins out loud
Finished an outline? That matters. Wrote 200 words? That matters. Acknowledge it — your brain needs proof that you're making progress.

7. Be deliberately kind to yourself
Eat something warm. Sleep before midnight at least a few nights. Remind yourself that done and imperfect beats perfect and never finished.

🌱 Recovery isn't a straight line, and you don't have to feel better all at once. Every small, gentle step you take today is already enough.

You've got this. 💙
